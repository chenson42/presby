## 0.48.1 — 2026-10-28

### Fix: setting up two-factor sign-in keeps your recovery codes on screen

**Value:** When you set up two-factor sign-in for the first time, your ten recovery codes now stay on the page until you choose Continue, and Continue takes you where you were going without asking for another code.

#### What's New
- **The codes stay on screen.** Before, they disappeared within a second of finishing setup, along with the Continue link.
- **Continue takes you on.** Finishing setup counts as confirming it's you for this sign-in. If that did not work for any reason, the page says you may be asked for one more code.
- **The codes are shown once.** If you reload or leave the page they are not shown again, so save them first. If you lose them, make a new set from the Two-Factor Auth page.
- **Making a new set now asks you to confirm first.** If you have not confirmed it's you in this sign-in, with your authenticator app or a recovery code, the page shows a short "Confirm it's you" step in place of the button and brings you back. This also applies to an account that has set up two-factor but is not required to use it.
- **Coming back to the setup page** with a destination now offers a Continue button.
- **What this does not change.** Setting up two-factor on an account that has not done so yet is still open to whoever is signed in to that account, as before, and now counts as confirmed straight away. If your connection drops at the very moment setup finishes, you may be set up without having seen your codes; confirm it's you, then make a new set. If an administrator resets someone's two-factor, a browser that is already signed in stays signed in until it signs out. Setup still has no limit on how many codes can be tried in a row.

**Notes for the operator:** no migration, no new setting. A recovery-codes cookie from before this update expires on its own; nothing reads it. **This is the fix that 0.48.0's hold on real sign-in invitations was waiting for.** Once 0.48.1 is deployed, walk one real invitee in production on a phone (invitation, account, sign in, set up two-factor, the ten codes stay on screen, Continue, landing in the congregation with no second code prompt). When that walk passes, real sign-in invitations can resume and 0.48.0's what's-new post can be published.

**Technical:** DECISION-292 (amends DECISION-230's call sites); findings F166 and F344 (`docs/schema-design-2.md` section 2aj). No migration.

## 0.48.0 — 2026-10-26

### Feature: sign-in invitations, and connecting a sign-in to a person's record

**Value:** Until now the only way to connect a sign-in to a person at a congregation was the platform's own invitation to a first administrator or treasurer. So a member of the Session who was recorded as an elder could not approve new members, because nobody could give them a way to sign in. A congregation's office can now invite a person it has already recorded to sign in, and a person who signs in with the email address on their record can be connected to it without an invitation.

#### What's New

- **Invite a person to sign in.** On a person's edit page there is a card, "Sign in to PresbyPortal". It shows the address on file, and Send emails that address a link that works once and expires in 7 days. Accepting connects the invitee's sign-in to that person's record. It adds nothing to the record: whatever role or seat the record already holds then takes effect. You can send again after five minutes, up to five times, or cancel an invitation that has not been accepted. People who can manage people can invite; to invite someone who holds a seat on the Session or the Board of Deacons you also need to be able to manage officers.
- **The invitee does not need to be told anything first.** The email names the sender and the congregation and says what the invitation is. Someone who is new chooses a password; someone who already has an account signs in, and the invitation can also be accepted on the site (below).
- **Connecting by signing in.** When someone signs in with an email address that is on exactly one person's record at a congregation, PresbyPortal connects that sign-in to the record, without an invitation. It does not do this for visitors, for an address two people share, for someone recorded at more than one congregation, or while an invitation to that address is waiting (they accept the invitation instead). A person who holds a role or a seat on the Session or the Board of Deacons is connected only after they confirm two-factor sign-in, after which they choose "Check again" on the page they land on. A congregation can turn this off under Features ("Connect people when they sign in"). That stops new connections and does not undo any.
- **Accepting on the site.** If an invitation was sent to the address you sign in with, you can accept it on the site after you sign in, without opening the email. The founding administrator and treasurer invitations always need two-factor sign-in first.
- **You can see where things stand.** A person's card says "Connected through an invitation" or "Connected through sign-in" with the date. The Community list shows "Invitation sent", "Invitation expired", "Email bounced" or "Not connected yet" beside a person, and, for people who can manage people, the Officers page shows the same beside each member of the Session.
- **A hand-off on Pending approvals.** When a congregation has a Session but none of its members has connected a sign-in, the Pending approvals page now lists each seat with its state and a link to invite or open, for people who can do that, and tells everyone else to ask an administrator. A member of the Session can approve once they have connected a sign-in, either by signing in with the email address on their record or by accepting an invitation.
- **Our staff side.** PresbyPortal staff can see the masked addresses of the invitations a congregation sent, and cancel all of an organization's open sign-in invitations if something goes wrong, separately from cancelling a first-administrator designation.

#### Things to know

- **The address you send to is the person.** Whoever opens the link becomes that person on PresbyPortal, so send it only to an address that belongs to them. We refuse an address that two people at your congregation share. If you change a person's email after sending, the earlier link stops working.
- **No take-back yet.** You can cancel an invitation that has not been accepted, but once someone accepts you cannot disconnect them yourself. If the wrong person accepts, send us a support ticket. Turning off "Connect people when they sign in" does not disconnect anyone either.
- **Visitors.** You can invite a visitor. A visitor who signs in can see your member directory and your calendar, with each person's privacy settings applied, the same as any member. We ask you to confirm before sending to a visitor, and we record that you did. The confirmation tells you what they will see; it does not limit it. We plan to give visitors a narrower view in a later release.
- **Who can invite whom.** If a person holds a seat on the Session or the Board of Deacons, only someone who can manage officers can invite them. You cannot invite someone who holds access you do not have yourself.
- **A residual we record but do not block.** Someone who can edit a person's email can point a record at an address they control. Anyone who then signs in with that address is connected to the record, and if the record holds a role or a seat they must first confirm two-factor sign-in, but we do not block it. Every edit and every connection is recorded so it can be reviewed.
- **Limits.** Each congregation can send up to 30 invitations a day, with at most 3 to the same address. People under 18 can be invited; a child who shares a parent's address needs their own address on file first.
- **Accounts now record whether we have confirmed the email address,** and changing the address starts that over. Two accounts can no longer share an address that differs only by capital letters.
- **Under the hood, for the record (no action needed).** A sign-in can be connected to a person from a congregation's side only when the person accepts an invitation, or signs in with a proven address that is on exactly one eligible record. PresbyPortal's own database access is not bound by that rule; no platform code makes such a connection, and a code check watches for one. A Google sign-in whose address differs only by capitals from an existing account is now refused instead of quietly creating a second account. Changing an existing account's email remains an application-level operation; moving it behind a stricter check is planned.

**For the operator.** This release has a migration, `0065`, and it must be applied to production **before** the code is merged. The steps, in order, are in `docs/deployment.md`, "Sign-in invitations and connect at sign-in (0065)": snapshot, re-run the pre-read counts (case-duplicate addresses, stamped accounts with an email change, open invitations, the deploy-day connect count, toggle rows, Google accounts), apply with `-1` on the direct endpoint, merge and deploy, verify against the catalog, then walk the real path and do the one-time Google sign-in check on a congregation made for testing. On 2026-10-25 the deploy-day count was 0, so no one is connected by this release on the day it ships; re-measure just before the apply, and if it is not 0 this note needs a line saying people "may be connected the next time they sign in".

**Hold real invitations until F166 ships (operator ruling, 2026-10-26).** Deploying this release sends nothing by itself, but a person who accepts an invitation as a new account enrols two-factor sign-in on the way in, and today that page does not show their recovery codes or a way to continue (F166). Do not send a real sign-in invitation, and do not tell a congregation to use them, until F166 is on `main`; the walk after deploy is yours, as operator, only. The what's-new post for this release is also held until then. Connecting by signing in is on by default for every congregation; switch it off per organization under Features if you want it held too. Two-factor sign-in becomes role-based in a separate pipeline (F343, not merged); when the second of the two merges, `docs/TODO.md` lists the walks to re-check.
