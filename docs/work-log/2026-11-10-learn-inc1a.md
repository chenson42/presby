## Operator-approved content (2026-11-11): the voicemail voice-cloning scenario (Q5, gap A1)

Approved as written; placed **last** (scenario 7 of 7); the rules card becomes **six rules**. Phase 3 carries this text verbatim into the content module (rewriting the other six under A2/A3 is separate).

- **Channel:** voicemail (new channel variant; rendered as a transcript, no player, no link). **From:** "Unknown caller" · "Voicemail · 0:41". No phone-shaped string (F439).
- **Body:** *(crying, out of breath)* "Grandma? Grandma, it's me. Please don't be mad. I was in a car accident and they're saying it was my fault, and I'm at the police station. My nose is broken, so I sound weird. Please don't call Mom and Dad, they'll kill me. There's a lawyer here, Mr. Grant. He's going to call you back in a few minutes and tell you what to do. Please, Grandma, I'm so scared. I love you."
- **isScam:** true
- **Verdict title:** This is the "grandchild in trouble" scam, now with a cloned voice.
- **Tells:**
  - The voice can sound exactly right. A few seconds of someone's voice from a video online is enough for AI to copy it. A voice that sounds like your grandchild no longer proves it's your grandchild.
  - It explains away anything that sounds off. "My nose is broken, so I sound weird" is there so you won't trust your doubts.
  - It asks you to keep it secret. "Don't call Mom and Dad" stops you from checking with the one person who could clear it up in a minute.
  - It's a crisis that only money fixes. A "lawyer" will call next asking for bail, often cash handed to a courier who comes to your door, or gift cards.
  - It's built to rush you. Fear and love are used so you act before you think.
- **Lesson:** Hang up and call your grandchild yourself, on the number you already have. If they don't answer, call their parents, even though the message told you not to. Then agree on a family code word that only your family knows, and ask for it whenever a call like this comes in. Police and courts never take bail in cash at your door or in gift cards.
- **Sixth rule:** "A familiar voice is not proof. Hang up and call back on a number you already have — and agree on a family code word."

## Data Model

**No tables, no columns, no indexes.** Migration `drizzle/0087_presby_learn.sql` (hand-written, idempotent, replays cleanly: drop-then-add, `create or replace`, revoke-then-grant) holds exactly three things:

1. **The category CHECK.** `alter table organization_feature_categories drop constraint if exists organization_feature_categories_category_check;` then `add constraint organization_feature_categories_category_check check (category in ('people','worship','giving','governance','reports','communications','learn'));`. The name is the live one the architect read from `pg_constraint` (the unnamed inline CHECK of 0040 takes it). `administration` stays refused. No backfill (a missing category row is ON, DECISION-130/330). The Drizzle `check()` in `src/lib/db/domain/org-feature-categories.ts` moves with it (and its "six-value" comments).
2. **`presby_learn_enabled(p_org, p_key)`, owner-only** (DECISION-331). Invoker, `stable`, `search_path` pinned. `revoke all ... from public, presby_app, presby_platform` and **no grant**: it is callable only from inside the definer below, where it runs as the owner (`neondb_owner`, `rolbypassrls = t`), which is why it can see the FORCE-RLS toggle and category tables with no GUC. Shape (the implementer finalizes; the literal-pin test parses exactly these two `CASE` pairs):

```sql
create or replace function presby_learn_enabled(p_org uuid, p_key text)
returns boolean language sql stable set search_path = public, pg_temp
as $function$
  select coalesce((
    select (k.org_types is null or o.organization_type::text = any (k.org_types))
       and (not coalesce((select f.enabled from feature_flags f
                           where f.key = 'org_portal.feature_categories'), false)
            or coalesce((select c.enabled from organization_feature_categories c
                          where c.organization_id = p_org and c.category = k.category), true))
       and coalesce((select t.enabled from organization_feature_toggles t
                      where t.organization_id = p_org and t.feature_key = p_key), false)
      from organizations o,
           lateral (select case p_key when 'org_portal.spot_the_scam' then 'learn' end        as category,
                           case p_key when 'org_portal.spot_the_scam' then null::text[] end   as org_types) k
     where o.id = p_org and k.category is not null
  ), false);
$function$;
```
   Closed key list: an unknown or null key, a non-Learn key (for example `org_portal.giving`) and an unknown organization all return `false`, so an anonymous caller can never use this to ask about any other toggle. Composition is exactly `orgFeatureEnabledInTx` (type, then axis and category, then toggle; axis off when its flag row is missing; category row missing is ON; toggle row missing is OFF).
3. **`presby_public_learn(p_slug, p_key)`, the entry point** (plpgsql, `#variable_conflict use_column`, SECURITY DEFINER, `grant execute ... to presby_app`, revoked from `public` and `presby_platform`). Always returns exactly one row. Gate order is careers': (a) the organization exists by slug and is `active`; (b) it has an `organization_sites` row with `status = 'live'`; (c) `presby_learn_enabled(org.id, p_key)`. Any failure returns `('unavailable')`; success returns `('available')`. It returns no organization name and no other column (the route already holds the name from the resolved site). Function comments state the collapse ("one answer for every cause"). Later static Learn pages call it unchanged; 1b's data-bearing definer will call the predicate; each later key extends the predicate's two `CASE` lists by `create or replace` in its own migration (Rule 16 serializes them).

- *ALTER/DROP dependencies:* only the CHECK is dropped. `grep` of every `drizzle/*.sql`: the only definitions that list the six category literals are `0040` (the CHECK) and the Drizzle mirror; the predicates of 0067, 0071, 0078 and 0085 read `organization_feature_categories` by a single literal each and are unaffected by a wider list; no `SECURITY DEFINER` body enumerates the six. ### The island (`scam-trainer.tsx`), behaviour spec

- **State:** `index`, `answered` (`boolean | null`, the visitor's guess that it is a scam), `score`, `done`. Same reducer shape as the prototype. Answering twice is ignored; `Next` advances once.
- **Imports (allow-list, pinned):** `react`, `lucide-react`, `@/components/ui/*` (`Button`), `@/components/shared/hydration-gate`, `@/lib/learn/spot-the-scam/content`, `@/lib/learn/spot-the-scam/copy`. Nothing else.
- **Forbidden in the file (pinned by T3):** `fetch`, `XMLHttpRequest`, `sendBeacon`, `WebSocket`, `'use server'`, a server-action import, `next/link`, `next/navigation`, `next/*` of any kind, `localStorage`, `sessionStorage`, `document.cookie`, `indexedDB`, `<a`, `href`, `window.open`, `location`, `dangerouslySetInnerHTML`, `getPlatformDb`.
- **No props.** It takes no URL and no organization. It reads the content module directly.
- **`HydrationGate`** wraps the controls, so a tap before hydration meets a visibly disabled control (the `disabled:bg-muted` styles of `Button`), never a lost tap (F440). The message text above them renders server-side and is readable with JavaScript off.
- **Focus management (A6; Phase 2 Note 4).** The pressed button unmounts on every step, so focus is moved deliberately, never left on `<body>`: after an answer, to the verdict heading (`tabIndex={-1}`); after "Next message" and after "Practice again", to the new "Message n of N" heading; after the last message, to the result heading. The effect skips its first run (a page load never steals focus). The verdict heading is the announcement; no `aria-live` region is added (a focus move and a live region together read twice).
- **Headings:** the frame owns an h2 ("Practice"); the island's "Message n of N" and the result heading are h3; the verdict line is h4. Ids come from `useId()` (a bundle may place the slot twice on one page).

### G6. The seven scenarios (for operator review)

One fictional world throughout: **Thistledown Presbyterian Church**, whose real addresses end in `thistledownpres.invalid`; Pastor Ellis Hartwell; Joan Pembry in the church office; a fictional vendor, Brightwater Supply (`brightwatersupply.invalid`). Every scam sender's look-alike is a hyphen swap of the real domain (`thistledown-pres.invalid`, `brightwater-supply.invalid`), except the generic alarm domain of scenario 5 (as in the prototype). No council, synod or presbytery is named anywhere; no real company or place (the prototype's grocery chain and gift-card brand are gone); no phone number or phone-shaped digits; the routing and account numbers are fictional, grouped, and invalid by construction. The prototype's teaching point for each scenario is kept; each change is listed at the end. Straight quotes and apostrophes in code.

**1. Email, scam** · From: `Pastor Ellis Hartwell` <`ellis.hartwell@thistledown-pres.invalid`> · Subject: `Quiet favor — please keep between us`
- `Hi, are you available? I need a discreet favor.`
- `A family at church is in crisis and I want to get them help today without embarrassing them. Could you buy four $100 gift cards at the grocery store and send me photos of the codes on the back? I will pay you back on Sunday.`
- `I'm heading into a hospital visit and can't take calls. Please do this quickly and keep it between us.`
- `Blessings, Pastor Ellis`
- Verdict: `This is the classic pastor gift-card scam.`
- Tells: `The address is a look-alike. Thistledown's real addresses end in thistledownpres.invalid. This one is thistledown-pres.invalid, with a hyphen added.` · `Gift cards as payment. No pastor will ever ask you to send gift-card codes. Ever.` · `Urgency plus secrecy: “today,” “can't take calls,” “keep it between us.” It is built so you won't check.` · `A reason you can't call to check is built right into the message.`
- Lesson: `Check through a second channel you already trust: call the pastor's real number or walk to the office. Never reply, and never buy the cards. AI now writes these fluently, so bad grammar is no longer a reliable tell.`

**2. Email, genuine** · From: `Thistledown Church Office` <`office@thistledownpres.invalid`> · Subject: `This week: potluck sign-up and session packet`
- `Friends,`
- `Two reminders for the week. The all-church potluck is Sunday after worship. The sign-up sheet is in the narthex, or reply to this email and Joan will add you.`
- `Elders: the session packet for Tuesday's meeting is attached, same as every month.`
- `See you Sunday!`
- Verdict: `This one is genuine.`
- Tells: `It comes from the church's real address, the one every other newsletter comes from.` · `It asks for nothing sensitive: no money, no codes, no passwords, no links to “verify” anything.` · `No urgency, no secrecy. It invites you to reply to a person you know.` · `It matches an expected rhythm: the monthly packet, the usual potluck.`
- Lesson: `Genuine church email is boring in the best way: an expected sender, an expected rhythm, nothing demanded. Caution is good, but the goal is confidence, not fear of every message.`

**3. Text, scam** · Header: `Text message · A number you don't have saved`
- `Hello, this is Pastor Ellis. I'm in a meeting and can't talk. Are you free? I need you to handle something for me right away. God bless.`
- Verdict: `This is an impersonation text, the opening move.`
- Tells: `A number you don't have saved, claiming to be your pastor. Scammers copy names from church websites.` · `“Can't talk right now”: the excuse not to check is built in before you can ask.` · `A vague, urgent favor. The real ask (usually gift cards or a wire) comes after you reply.` · `A slightly off tone. AI lets scammers copy a pastor's style convincingly, but the situation itself is the tell.`
- Lesson: `Don't reply, even to say no. A reply tells them your number works. Text or call the pastor at the number you already have. Then warn the people around you at church or work; these arrive in waves.`

**4. Email, scam (business email compromise)** · From: `Brightwater Supply Billing` <`billing@brightwater-supply.invalid`> · Subject: `URGENT: updated banking details for your invoice`
- `Dear Treasurer,`
- `Our payment account has changed as part of a banking transition. Effective immediately, please send this month's invoice payment to the new account below.`
- `Routing: 9900 00001 · Account: 0000 1234 5678`
- `This must be completed before Friday to avoid a late fee and a hold on your church's account. Do not reply to this email; it is monitored by our audit partner.`
- Verdict: `This is business email compromise, aimed at your treasurer.`
- Tells: `“Updated banking details” is the single most expensive scam a church treasurer will face.` · `The address is a look-alike. Brightwater's earlier invoices came from brightwatersupply.invalid; this one is brightwater-supply.invalid. You would only catch it by comparing with an invoice you already have.` · `A deadline plus a threat (a late fee and a hold) to rush you past checking.` · `“Do not reply” closes off the easy way to check.`
- Lesson: `Any change to where money goes gets checked by phone, using a number you already have on file, never one from the email. A real vendor will happily wait a day while you check.`

**5. Email, scam (credential phishing)** · From: `Church Email System` <`no-reply@mailbox-quota-alerts.invalid`> · Subject: `Action required: your mailbox will be deactivated in 24 hours`
- `Your mailbox storage is 99% full. Incoming messages will be rejected within 24 hours.`
- `To keep your account active, verify your mailbox now:`
- `[A large button reads: KEEP MY ACCOUNT ACTIVE]`
- `This is an automated message from your email provider.`
- Verdict: `This is credential phishing: it is after your password.`
- Tells: `A countdown and a threat: lose your email unless you act now.` · `The sender is a generic, alarm-sounding address, not your actual email provider.` · `One big button whose whole job is to take you to a fake sign-in page.` · `It's addressed to no one. Real providers usually name you and your account.`
- Lesson: `Never sign in through a link in an alarming email. Go to your email provider directly, the way you always do. If your account were really full, you would see it there. This is how church accounts get taken over, then used to scam everyone who trusts the sender.`

**6. Email, genuine** · From: `Thistledown Supper Series` <`suppers@thistledownpres.invalid`> · Subject: `Registration open: fall supper series (free)`
- `Friends,`
- `Registration is open for our free fall supper series, “Stories from the Mission Field,” on Wednesday evenings this fall. Sign up on our website under Events, or reply and we'll add you.`
- `Each evening's slides will be posted afterward, as always, on the church website.`
- `Grace and peace,`
- Verdict: `This one is genuine.`
- Tells: `The sender matches the church's real address and its normal voice.` · `Nothing is demanded: no deadline pressure, no payment, no sign-in link.` · `It points you to the website you already know, not to a special button.` · `“As always” fits an established pattern you can check for yourself.`
- Lesson: `When in doubt, go there yourself: type the address or use your bookmark instead of tapping anything. A genuine invitation survives that detour. A scam depends on you not taking it.`

**7. Voicemail, scam (operator-approved 2026-11-11, verbatim, last)** · Header: `Voicemail · 0:41` · From: `Unknown caller` · direction (italic): `(crying, out of breath)`
- Transcript: `"Grandma? Grandma, it's me. Please don't be mad. I was in a car accident and they're saying it was my fault, and I'm at the police station. My nose is broken, so I sound weird. Please don't call Mom and Dad, they'll kill me. There's a lawyer here, Mr. Grant. He's going to call you back in a few minutes and tell you what to do. Please, Grandma, I'm so scared. I love you."`
- Verdict: `This is the "grandchild in trouble" scam, now with a cloned voice.`
- Tells (the approved five): `The voice can sound exactly right. A few seconds of someone's voice from a video online is enough for AI to copy it. A voice that sounds like your grandchild no longer proves it's your grandchild.` · `It explains away anything that sounds off. "My nose is broken, so I sound weird" is there so you won't trust your doubts.` · `It asks you to keep it secret. "Don't call Mom and Dad" stops you from checking with the one person who could clear it up in a minute.` · `It's a crisis that only money fixes. A "lawyer" will call next asking for bail, often cash handed to a courier who comes to your door, or gift cards.` · `It's built to rush you. Fear and love are used so you act before you think.`
- Lesson: `Hang up and call your grandchild yourself, on the number you already have. If they don't answer, call their parents, even though the message told you not to. Then agree on a family code word that only your family knows, and ask for it whenever a call like this comes in. Police and courts never take bail in cash at your door or in gift cards.`

**What changed from the prototype, scenario by scenario (all to meet A2, A3, F439):** (1) the pastor, the church and the domain are invented; the look-alike is a hyphen swap; the grocery chain and the gift-card brand are generic; "the pastor's real address" tell now names both domains so the reader has something to compare.
