## Program map

### What exists today (do not rebuild)

Online giving shipped as v0.34.0, with migrations 0055/0056 and 18 tables:
- Stripe Connect and the multi-provider `giving_provider` enum, which holds only `stripe` today.
- The treasurer's gift desk: cash, check and stock gifts recorded offline by `giving.record`.
- Donors, funds, and itemized payouts.
- Frozen year-end PDF statements, gated behind `GIVING_STATEMENTS_ENABLED` until a CPA reviews the wording.
- The treasurer designation, `giving.view/record/manage` (tier 2, `requires_two_factor`), and the append-only `giving_gift_reversals`.

There is **no ledger and no reimbursement code**. The `ledger.approve` permission row is a key nobody holds (S3). Two facts shape everything below:
1. `giving_funds` already carries `restriction_class`. A ledger fund and a giving fund must be one concept, not two.
2. Statements already read `giving_gifts`, so any gift that lands in that table (imported history included) flows into a statement for free.

### Recommended order and why

1. **Increment 1: Reimbursements, request through paid, no ledger.** It is the only treasury feature in the hard-cutover definition ("if deferred it is not used", and FPCW uses it). It has no dependency on anything unbuilt. The prior-art spec is complete, and it is much smaller than giving: roughly 6–8 tables against giving's 18.
2. **Increment 2: Giving continuity.** It is the only treasury work with an external calendar deadline, because statements are due every January. It turns on Q4. If giving moves to presby at cutover, this ships before cutover. If gifts stay in Vanco through 2027, it can sit behind the ledger but must land before the January 2028 statement run.
3. **Increment 3: Contribution entry depth.** It comes before the ledger because a counted-offering batch is the unit a bank deposit reconciles against. Designing the ledger first would force a retrofit.
4. **Increments 4–6: Ledger core, then posting and bank, then budgets and reports.** Each is post-cutover new product. Increment 4 gets its own program-level Phase 1/2 first, because the `schema-design-2.md` §7 sketch (double-entry lines, restriction on the fund) differs from the Lions model (cash-basis, single-entry with fund tagging).

**Why Increment 1 includes "record payment" although the TODO says Track R stops at `approved`.** The TODO line reflects that the treasury half belongs to Track E. Under the hard-cutover rule, though, fpcw's treasurer records payment today, and a presby with no `approved to paid` step strands every approved request or sends the treasurer back to paper. The reconciling fact is that fpcw's payment step needs no ledger. Increment 1 therefore records a payment, not a posting. The Lions review found the opposite trap: a paid reimbursement posted with no bank account or check number could never be reconciled. So the payment record must carry method, reference, date and (later) the account it came from. See Q1.

### Pass 2 — Flows

Suggested routes (design may differ): member surface `/o/<slug>/reimbursements`; treasurer surface `/o/<slug>/admin/reimbursements`.

**Flow 1 — File a request (phone, 360px).** Entry is the portal tile "Request a reimbursement".
1. Pick the type: "Pay me back", "Pay someone else back" (needs `request_for_others`), or "Pay a vendor".
2. Pick a committee from the chargeable list, plus an expense category if that committee has any.
3. Enter the amount: typed digits become cents, with at most two decimals, and nothing is rounded silently.
4. Enter the expense date and the purpose.
5. Name who pre-approved it. This is a recorded fact, not a workflow step (fpcw's ruling).
6. Attach receipts (library, camera and files all offered; never `capture=`), or tick "I can't attach a receipt".
7. Review, then Submit.
- Outcome: "Sent to your committee for approval", or "Your committee has nobody else who can approve, so this went to the treasurer."
- Failure: field errors come from the server and sit next to the field. A failed upload names the file and the reason in plain words, and the form keeps everything entered. A network or database failure says "We couldn't save that. Your entries are still here. Try again." A double-tap on Submit does not create a second request (idempotency key).

**Flow 2 — Approve or return.** Entry is the emailed link, which goes through `/launch?next=…`, sign-in and 2FA if required.
1. Open the request. It shows amount, payee, purpose, pre-approver, receipts, and flags (entered on someone's behalf; receipt reused on an earlier request).
2. Approve (a confirmation dialog states the amount) or Return, with a reason and a kind.
- Outcome: the requester and treasurer are notified.
- Failure:
  - Not an eligible seat: the same neutral not-found as a missing request.
  - Already decided: "This was already approved on <date>." No actor name is shown to a non-treasurer.
  - The request changed since the approver opened it: "This request changed after you opened it. Review it again." Approve carries the revision it displayed.

**Flow 3 — Treasurer pays.** Entry is the "Waiting for payment" queue.
1. Open the request and confirm there is a receipt or a recorded waiver.
2. Record payment: method (check, ACH, cash, other), reference (a check number), paid-on date, and an optional note.
3. Confirm.
- Outcome: status becomes `paid` and the requester is told "Paid" with no payment details.
- Failure:
  - The payer is the approver: "A different person must record this payment." The block is explicit and shown before the form.
  - No receipt and no waiver: blocked, with the reason named.
  - Wrong amount seen on the receipt: Return to requester. This clears the approval, and a fresh approval is required.

**Flow 4 — Correct a payment.** The treasurer chooses "Reverse payment", gives a reason, and the request returns to `approved`. Both payment rows remain visible. Nothing is deleted.

**Flow 5 — Requester follow-up.** The "My requests" list shows human statuses: Draft, Waiting for approval, Approved (waiting for payment), Paid, Sent back to you, Not approved, Withdrawn. Sent back to you opens edit-and-resubmit. Not approved is terminal except for withdrawing.

**Flow 6 — First-time setup.** An `org_features.manage` holder turns Reimbursements on. The treasurer picks the chargeable committees and optional categories. Empty states:
- No chargeable committee: the request form says "No committee can receive requests yet. Your treasurer chooses which groups can." The treasurer sees a link to the picker.
- Nobody holds a pay key: the toggle page and the queue say a treasurer must be designated through PresbyPortal support. This is the `GivingForbidden` dead-end lesson, applied now.
- Empty queues: "Nothing is waiting."

**Flow 7 — Toggle off with requests open.** A confirm dialog says "N requests are waiting. Turning this off hides the pages; nothing is deleted." Turning it back on restores everything.

**Flow 8 — Print and export.** The PDF is a one-request print of the form. It uses the existing `pdfkit` dependency, so there is no new dependency. The CSV is gated on `view`, audited as a bulk tier-2 export (as in giving), with a row cap and the formula-injection guard.

### Pass 3 — Permissions and Flags

**New tenant-catalog keys** (migration, never `src/lib/permissions.ts`; tier 2 is "financial"). The DECISION-078 test is applied per office and role.

| Key | Gate | Default binding and DECISION-078 verdict | 2FA |
|---|---|---|---|
| `reimbursement.request` | file and track my own requests | baseline `member` template. This is a policy choice (fpcw ruled open requesting in DECISION-130), not an office duty. The risk is visitors via F177, so Phase 3 must close it with an engagement check or defer the binding (Q8). | no |
| `reimbursement.request_for_others` | enter on someone's behalf | **no default**. Granted to named people or a custom role, as fpcw did for its treasurer. | no |
| `reimbursement.view` | read every request, receipts, CSV | `treasurer` template. Receiving and reporting funds is the constitutional job (DECISION-080/170 precedent). | **yes** (bulk tier-2 export) |
| `reimbursement.pay` | record and reverse payment, waive receipt, return-from-approved, void, override-approve | `treasurer` template only | **yes** (money leaves) |
| `reimbursement.manage` | chargeable groups, categories | `treasurer` template | Phase 3 decides |

Roles that fail the test for any money key: `founding_administrator` (tier 2 must not ride on the person who administers the site), `stated_clerk`, `session_member` (a collective body), and `installed_pastor`. The seat holder needs no catalog key (see below).

**Approval authority is a seat, not a flat key (analyst position; the architect rules in Phase 2).** In fpcw the flat approve permission ended up bound to every member, so the committee seat was the only real control and the flat key was vestigial. Presby has a precedent for authority held by a seat or time-boxed record and checked inside a `SECURITY DEFINER` function that derives actor, org and committee from the row: `care_grants` (schema-design-2 §8). I recommend the same. A request's approver is "an active member holding an approving seat on the committee the request is charged to, who is not the requester, creator or payee". The queue and tile predicate is a definer, like fpcw's `hasAnyApproverSeat`. The cost is that the seat has no place for `requires_two_factor`. That is acceptable here, because the independent payer, not the approver, is the money control.

**Treasurer role must be sufficient on its own** (westervillelions DECISION-115, the treasurer-self-sufficiency review). Phase 3 needs:
- A role-bundle test: a person who holds only the designated `treasurer` role can complete every treasurer verb above.
- A backfill of the new keys to existing org-owned treasurer roles minted by designation. The backfill is limited to designation-minted copies (the DECISION-199 shape) and is never a wildcard.

**Flag.** None new. The operator's standing rule is a per-org toggle, so the plan is a toggle-only `ORG_FEATURE_CATALOG` entry (suggested key `org_portal.reimbursement`, `flag: null`, `provisionedOn: false`), `orgTypes` congregation and new worshiping community (Q11), category `giving`.

**Gate order:** org toggle, then permission or seat. A missing toggle row means off and returns HTTP 200 with an "isn't turned on" page, never a 404 (the giving precedent).

**Rollback.** The change is additive. Turning the toggle off hides every surface and retains all rows. There is no destructive migration to reverse.

### Pass 4 — Edge cases the request did not mention

1. **2FA gate.** Treasurer keys carry `requires_two_factor`, so a person who holds one and is not enrolled is pushed to `/totp` by the existing proxy rule on `/o/*`. The emailed deep link must survive that round trip and land on the request. Phase 5 needs an e2e check with an MFA-enrolled seeded user, because the feature touches the sign-in path.
2. **Audit.** The request-level history is an append-only event table (who, when, from-status, to-status, note). Separately, `audit_events` rows are required for: approve; payment recorded and reversed; receipt waived; return-from-approved; void by a non-requester; treasurer override-approval; every CSV export; chargeable-group and category changes; and toggle changes. The `check:audit` tripwire must find an `AUDIT_ACTIONS` key in every action file. Audit metadata carries ids and counts, never amounts, purposes or names.
3. **Empty states.** All listed under Flow 6.
4. **Failure microcopy.** Human, with retained form state. No stack traces. The approver's "already decided" and "changed since you opened it" messages are required, not optional.
5. **360px.** Approve, Return and Record payment must be visible without horizontal scroll. The Lions treasury shipped an Approve button off-screen on a phone table and had to hot-fix it (westervillelions DECISION-114 era). Lists become cards at 360px. Receipt input must not set `capture=`, which hides "Photo Library" on iOS. Today no tripwire enforces that, so add one (fpcw has one).
6. **Departing people.**
   - An approver whose seat ends mid-queue is reflected live, because the check is re-derived on every action.
   - A requester whose membership ends before payment: an approved request stays payable. New requests need an active membership.
   - A merged person follows `merged_into_id`.
7. **Payee identity.** Person references are composite to `memberships`, never a bare `person_id` (schema-design-2 §9 item 3a). A vendor or non-member payee is a name column.
8. **Dates.** The expense date is the org-local date, using the existing time-zone derivation with its `America/New_York` fallback (F132). A future date is refused. Phase 3 sets a floor, with a one-year default in the analyst's proposal.
9. **Concurrency.** Two approvers acting at once: one wins and the other gets the "already approved" message. The committee cannot be changed after submit (otherwise a requester could re-aim a request at a friendlier committee). A retired committee keeps its existing requests.
10. **Notices.** Email carries no amounts and no free text, because the purpose field can hold sensitive content such as pastoral-care spending (the `check:giving-boundaries` B5 pattern). Recipient addresses come only through `contact-methods/reader.ts`, per the work-over-personal rule that Track C extended to notifications. A person with no deliverable address gets in-app only.
11. **Retention and erasure.** Records are kept at least 7 years (the giving precedent). Nothing is purged in v1. An erasure request on a person is answered the way giving's G13 answers it, by retaining the financial row.
12. **Duplicate protection.** The same receipt bytes attached to an earlier non-void request in the org raise a flag shown to the approver and treasurer only. The requester sees only their own history, so the flag cannot leak another person's request. A similar-amount-and-date warning on the form is a Phase 3 option.
13. **Fixtures.** Receipt test files and payees are synthetic bytes and invented names (No Real Data; the Lions PII scrub is the cautionary tale).
14. **Hydration.** Every `useForm` root renders `HydrationGate`, and no native dialogs are used: void and return use `AlertDialog`.

#### DECISION-267: Reimbursements are one module with a state machine enforced in the database

- **Six tables in 0072:**
  - `reimbursement_chargeable_groups` and `reimbursement_categories` (configuration, soft-ended).
  - `reimbursement_requests`.
  - `reimbursement_attachments` (soft-detached, and only while the request is `draft` or `returned`).
  - `reimbursement_payments` (insert-only).
  - `reimbursement_events` (insert-only history).
- **Reads go through definers, not tenancy-only RLS.** Giving's `self.ts` accepted that RLS hands every member every row, with the self-scoping left as paper. Here the purpose field can hold pastoral-care spending, and the duplicate-receipt flag must not leak, so the D23 "controlled read, not a policy" idiom applies instead.
  - There is a closed set of read definers: mine, waiting-for-me, the treasurer list, detail, attachment lookup, the CSV rows, and the count for the tile and badge.
  - Each takes the bounded actor claim `p_user_id`, bound server-side from the session (the 0062 `presby_roll__actor_holds` shape).
  - Each applies one viewer check:
    - the creator, for drafts;
    - the beneficiary or creator, from submitted onward;
    - a currently eligible seat holder, for submitted, returned and decided rows of that group;
    - a holder of `reimbursement.view`, for anything that is not a draft.
  - A missing request and a forbidden one return the same outcome.
- **All writes go through definers.**
  - An identity refusal raises one uniform literal. State refusals come back as a closed list of outcomes, pinned to a TypeScript constant by a source-scan test (the DECISION-188/198 style).
  - The lock order is the request row `FOR UPDATE`.
  - Audit rows are written in the same transaction from TypeScript (`audit-tx.ts`). Their metadata carries ids and counts only.
- **Transition table enforced by trigger.**
  - Proposed: `draft→submitted|withdrawn`; `submitted→approved|returned|not_approved|withdrawn|voided`; `returned→submitted|withdrawn|voided`; `not_approved→withdrawn`; `approved→paid|returned|withdrawn|voided`; `paid→approved` (only through a payment reversal, in the same transaction).
  - Phase 3 may rename states but must keep the table enforced by trigger.
  - An insert must be born `draft`, except for `origin='import'` rows (below).
  - After submit, the amount, group, kind, beneficiary and payee freeze.
  - `approved→returned` clears the approval-in-force columns; the event row keeps the history.
- **Ready for the ledger (R8).**
  - A payment row carries method (`check|ach|cash|other`), reference, paid-on date, an amount snapshot (the trigger checks it equals the request amount), the payer, and `unique (id, organization_id)` so a future journal entry can composite-FK to it.
  - A reversal is a new row with `offsets_payment_id`, one per payment (partial unique index), and a reason.
  - No fund or account column now. "Assign at pay time" is an additive column in Increment 5.
- **Idempotent submit.**
  - Every request is born a draft carrying a client key, with `unique (organization_id, created_by_user, client_key)`. Creation inserts with `on conflict do nothing` and then reads the row back.
  - Submit is a transition on that id. A second tap returns `already_submitted`, which TypeScript treats as success.
  - The revision is incremented by trigger on every edit or attachment change. Approve passes `p_expected_revision`; a mismatch returns `changed_since_viewed`.
- **R13 import door.** An `origin` column (`app|import`) whose CHECKs admit null actors only for `import`. The importer is a later owner-only definer (Track K) and is not built here.
- **0073** adds the five keys to the catalog (`on conflict do update`), the treasurer template bindings, the backfill (DECISION-269) and nothing else.

#### DECISION-268: Approval is a seat checked live in a definer, not a key; separation of duties is in the database

- **The seat rule** is one internal function. All of these must hold:
  - the actor's live, unmerged person is an active member of the org;
  - they hold a `group_memberships` row on the request's group, with `group_role in ('chair','leader')`, in date, on a group whose `membership_source='managed'`;
  - they are not the beneficiary, creator or payee.
- It is checked on every approve and return, so a seat that ends mid-queue loses authority immediately.
- **Derived groups (Session, Deacons)** have no chair or leader rows, so they fall to the override path (operator Q5). A future `deacons_moderator` mapping (parish pipeline, DECISION-242) is an additive change inside that same function. No speculative column now.
- **Override** requires `reimbursement.pay` (checked with `presby_has_permission` inside the definer) and a live finding that no eligible seat holder exists. It is stamped `approval_path='override'`.
- **Triggers that bind the owner too:**
  - On `→approved`, the approver is not the beneficiary, creator or payee.
  - On a payment insert, the payer is not the approver currently in force. The trigger itself derives `self_paid := payer in (beneficiary, creator)`; the caller never supplies it.
- **Sole-holder exception (operator Q7).** "Requester may pay only when they are the sole pay holder" needs a count of holders, so it lives in the definer as `needs_another_payer`. That makes it a definer property, not a trigger property, and that limit should be named.
- **The 2FA gap is accepted.** The seat has no `requires_two_factor`. The money control is the independent payer, whose keys require 2FA.
- **Laundering is unrepresentable by schema.** Kind `reimburse` has one beneficiary column, which is the payee. Kind `vendor` requires `vendor_name` and has no person payee. A creator who is not the beneficiary must hold `request_for_others`, checked in the definer.

#### DECISION-270: Receipts are private blobs served only through their request; `sharp`; a 4 MB cap; an upload route handler

**Depends on the F289 fix.** It needs its own bug-fix pipeline, now. That pipeline records its own decision; the shape I rule as the answer shared with careers:

- `blob_assets.audience` is a closed vocabulary: `public_site` or `private`.
  - The column is immutable by trigger.
  - The dedup key becomes `(organization_id, audience, content_hash)`.
  - `store()` takes `audience` as a required argument, with no default.
  - The public asset route resolves through a new `resolvePublic()` that adds `audience='public_site'`.
  - Rows referenced by `ticket_messages`, `giving_statements` or `people.photo_key` are backfilled as `private`; every other row becomes `public_site`.
  - Private consumers reference blobs through a composite FK that includes a constant `'private'` column, so a private consumer can only ever point at a private row. That is a database property.
- **Sequencing.** That migration must sort before the first migration that creates a private-blob consumer: treasury's schema migration, and careers' Increment 2. It must also ship before `GIVING_STATEMENTS_ENABLED` is set in production.
- **Careers.** The audience vocabulary may be extended by careers' Phase 2 (a non-deduping erasable class plus a byte-destroy path; F290). Treasury needs neither, because receipts are retained for 7 years.
- **Receipt bytes route** (`GET …/receipts/[attachmentId]`):
  - The URL carries the attachment row id. The blob key never reaches a URL or a client component.
  - The handler runs `auth()`, then `resolveOrgContext`, then the toggle, then the viewer-check definer, then `resolve()`.
  - Response headers: `nosniff`, `Cache-Control: private, no-store`, `Content-Security-Policy: sandbox; default-src 'none'`, `attachment` for PDF and `inline` for re-encoded images, and a server-chosen filename.
  - An unauthorized request and a missing one get the same 404.
  - A blob `UPDATE` guard for referenced receipts follows the 0056 statement-guard pattern.
- **Size (F299).**
  - Each file is at most 4 MB, with at most 5 per request. The 10 MB adapter bound cannot be used on Vercel.
  - The client downscales photos before upload.
  - The server re-encodes every image with `image-sanitize.ts`. That server step is the guarantee; the client step exists for usability.
- **Upload is a POST route handler, not a server action.** Raising `serverActions.bodySizeLimit` is global and would widen every action, including anonymous public-site ones.
  - This is the first cookie-authenticated POST route handler in the tenant tree, so `upload-request.ts` must enforce:
    - `Origin` belongs to the validated platform origin;
    - multipart only, with exactly one file part;
    - a `Content-Length` precheck, and a streamed byte count that aborts over the cap;
    - a magic-byte sniff (png, jpeg, webp, pdf).
  - Upload is allowed only to the creator, only while the request is `draft` or `returned`.
- **Recorded residuals:**
  - `store()` and the attach definer run in separate transactions, so an attach failure can leave an orphaned private blob. No route can reach it.
  - Receipts stored as bytea grow the database (roughly 1 GB a year at fpcw scale). This is a watch item; the adapter interface already allows an object-store implementation later.
- **Duplicate flag.** The attachment row stores the sha256 of the bytes as received, before re-encoding. Duplicate detection joins on that column, never on `blob_assets` (DECISION-030). Only the treasurer and approvers see the flag, through the read definers.
