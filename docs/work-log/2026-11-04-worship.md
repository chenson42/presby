## Prior art: what FPCW's worship module actually is

The module is in `fpcw-directory`: `src/lib/worship/*`, `src/app/api/worship/*`, and `src/lib/db/schema.ts` lines 2760-2900 and 1447-1480. It is **liturgical role scheduling, attendance and sermon transcripts. It has no songs, music, chord sheets, bulletins or CCLI.** It covers roughly a third of the operator's request.

| FPCW piece | What it does | Verdict for presby |

## Reference: Planning Center Services

Verified from https://planningcenter.com/services and https://planningcenter.com/integrations, fetched 2026-11-04. The functional model to borrow:

- **Service Type, then Plan (dated), then Items.** Items are songs, headers, media, sermon, announcements and communion, with notes and colors.
- **Teams, then Positions, then Plan People.** A person is scheduled to a position and responds. They can also set **blockout dates**, and a leader can see who is blocked.
- **Reminders, templates, auto-schedule** (by last-served, preference and blockout), signup sheets, and household preferences. An "Email my leader" button.
- **Song library** with arrangements, keys, tags and attachments (chord charts, PDFs, audio). A media player supports parts and loops. Music Stand is a separate annotation app.
- **CCLI reporting** is automatic from plans.
- **SongSelect integration** "allows you to import lyrics and chord charts from *your* SongSelect account" (the church's own account). PraiseCharts, RehearsalMix and RehearsalPack (MultiTracks) are the audio and chart sources.
- **Pricing is per person who accesses the system.** The free tier is 5 people and 100 MB. This is the competitive bar: presby's value is that it is bundled with the roll, directory and groups.
- Their public API is at https://developer.planning.center/docs/#/apps/services. The URL returns 200. I did not read the single-page-app content. An importer is plausible, but that is an assumption.

Do not copy Planning Center's tier model. They bill by seat, and presby does not.

## CCLI and licensing: verified vs assumed

I fetched these pages on 2026-11-04. The statements below are "CCLI says", not legal advice.

**Verified**

| Fact | Source |

| The Church Copyright License covers: projecting lyrics, printing lyrics in bulletins, programs and song sheets, storing song files on computers for projection, recording services for limited distribution, custom arrangements only where no published version exists, and translations. | https://ccli.com/us/en/church-copyright-license, https://ccli.com/us/en/church-copyright-license-summary |
| **Every reproduction must carry the title, writer credits, the © line, and "Used by Permission. CCLI License #…".** | License summary |

| **Not permitted:** distributing copies outside of church services, duplicating choral, instrumental or keyboard arrangements, altering lyrics or melody, and charging a fee. The program grants **no public performance rights**. Accompaniment tracks and artist recordings are not covered by the base license. | License summary |
| The **Rehearsal License** is a separate add-on. It lets a music director and authorized personnel stream, download or copy commercial recordings to worship leaders, musicians and vocalists "from your Church's proprietary website, a hosted website or a streaming service", **as temporary reference copies only**. Recording your own version for distribution and retaining copies for other uses are prohibited. Each distribution is reportable. | https://ccli.com/us/en/rehearsal-license-summary, https://ccli.com/us/en/reporting |
| **SongSelect** (a separate subscription, available to license holders) gives chord sheets, lead sheets, vocal sheets, transposition and ChordPro download (Premium). Its package table lists "3rd-party Integration". "A single subscription serves your entire worship and media team." | https://ccli.com/us/en/songselect |
| **Auto-reporting exists only through "participating" software** that CCLI "partnered with". It covers the Church Copyright and Streaming licenses from setlists. Rehearsal and Streaming Plus are not yet automatic. Manual reporting is at reporting.ccli.com, with a CCLI Profile and an access code. | https://ccli.com/us/en/auto-reporting, https://ccli.com/us/en/reporting |
| CCLI sells its own rehearsal tool ("Rehearse") and links multitracks through Loop Community. These are adjacent products, not partners. | https://ccli.com/us/en/rehearse |

**Not verified. These are Phase 2 and operator items, not facts.**

- **Whether CCLI has a public developer API or a partner program.** I found none: nothing in the US sitemap, and guessed URLs (`/partners`, `/developers`, `/api`, `/integrations`) returned 404. The sentence "partnered with the leading software providers" suggests a business agreement, not a self-serve API. **Treat CCLI integration as something we must request from CCLI, with an unknown outcome.**
- The Master Terms of Agreement (the "HERE" link on the license summary) was not read. SongSelect's Terms of Use page (https://songselect.ccli.com/about/termsofuse) renders by JavaScript and returned nothing I could read. So **whether a church may store SongSelect charts in a third-party hosted app is unverified.**
- Reporting cadence: the license summary says a copy report is due "once every 2 1/2 years" when notified. The reporting page says "ideally weekly" for manual reporters. The two CCLI pages disagree.
- **OneLicense** (onelicense.net) returned 403 to my fetch. For Presbyterian congregations, OneLicense (which, to my knowledge, covers mainline hymnals such as the PC(USA) hymnal) may matter as much as CCLI. That is unverified background knowledge. Q2 asks which licenses the congregations hold.
- Whether Planning Center's SongSelect integration is a public API or a private partner arrangement.

**What this means for the platform:**

1. We can build now with no CCLI access: CCLI song number, writers and the © line on every song, the notice string, and a "songs used this period" report-ready list for manual entry.
2. SongSelect import and auto-reporting need CCLI's agreement. Plan them as an optional later increment.
3. Hosting files makes the platform a conduit for a church's licensed copies, under a license that cannot be assigned and that requires destruction on lapse. See gaps G9 to G12.

## Flows

**Flow 1: Set up a team (director, once).** Worship, then Teams, then New team. Pick a type (choir or team, both existing global group types). Name it. Pick a starter set of positions or add your own. Add people from the congregation (this is the existing group add). Mark which positions each person can fill. Outcome: the team has a roster and positions.
- Failure: no permission gives "You don't have permission to manage worship teams" with a link to ask the administrator. A person with no record at this congregation is not in the picker. A duplicate position name is refused.

**Flow 2: Plan a Sunday (director or leader).** Worship, then Services, then New service (or generate the next N weeks from a recurring service type). The service is created as a calendar event of a worship type, so the calendar, cancel, and members-only behavior all work. Pick which teams serve. The positions appear as open slots.
- Failure: a duplicate service at the same date and time warns. A cancelled service shows cancelled, with its schedule retained and not notified again.

**Flow 3: Schedule people (leader).** Open the service, then an open position. The picker lists the team roster filtered to those who can fill that position and are not blocked out. Pick a person. The request is sent (email, Inc 1) and the slot shows Requested. Outcome: Confirmed, Declined (with the slot reopened and the leader notified), or still waiting (with a leader-visible unconfirmed flag and a reminder).
- Failure: a person with no usable address shows "No way to reach them, confirm by phone", and the leader can record the response. Scheduling a blocked-out person warns but allows an override. A double booking (same person, same time, another position) warns.

**Flow 4: Respond to a request (team member).** Email: "You're asked to serve as {Position} on {Date}." The member signs in (deep link, with the 2FA challenge if their account needs it) and lands on that request. Accept, or Decline with an optional note. The confirmation says what happens next.
- Failure: the service was cancelled or the request withdrawn shows "This request is no longer open" with a link to my schedule. Responding twice is idempotent. A signed-in account that is not the addressee sees the same neutral message as an unknown link.

**Flow 5: Blockout dates (team member).** My schedule, then "Dates I can't serve", then a date or range, with an optional reason visible to leaders only.
- Failure: an overlapping range merges. An end date before the start date is refused. A blockout that falls on an already-confirmed assignment says "You're already confirmed for {date}. Tell your leader", and does not silently cancel it.

**Flow 6: Order of service and songs (Inc 2).** Open the plan, then Add item: song, scripture, prayer, sermon, announcement or note. A song pick shows the key and the license notice. The ordered list is published to the team, then to the members.
- Failure: an unpublished plan shows the member surface "The plan isn't published yet". A song with no CCLI number is flagged as "can't be reported".

**Flow 7: Open a chart or play a recording on a phone (Inc 5).** Home, then Worship, then this Sunday, then a song, then the chart (or the audio). It opens on a 360px screen with the key shown and optional transposition.
- Failure: no network (stage environments often have none) shows "Can't load right now" and does not cache anything for offline. A removed file or lapsed license shows "This file is no longer available" without saying which. A non-team viewer gets the identical not-found response.

**Flow 8: Rehearsal and team message (Inc 4).** The leader creates a rehearsal (a calendar event tied to the team) and the members RSVP. The leader sends a message to the team, or to just those scheduled for a given service.
- Failure: a recipient with do-not-contact set is skipped, and the leader sees "N people were not sent this because they asked not to be contacted". A send that fails midway reports how many got it.

**Flow 9: Bulletin (Inc 3).** Open the published plan, then Bulletin. The order of worship is prefilled with songs, readings and the licensing notice. Add announcements (typed text, with upcoming events pullable from the calendar). Preview. Print, or save as PDF, with a large-print option. Optionally publish a "website copy" to the public site.
- Failure: a missing required notice blocks printing a song that lacks a © line, with a plain-language reason. Any text drawn from tier-2 or tier-3 data is never auto-inserted.

**Flow 10: CCLI report prep (Inc 2), then connection (Inc 6, conditional).** Worship, then Reports, then "Songs used": date range, song, CCLI number, count, and rehearsal-file distributions (Inc 5). Export to enter at reporting.ccli.com. In Inc 6, connect CCLI so reporting is automatic.
- Failure: songs missing a CCLI number are listed separately as "can't be reported".

- **Seat-based authority (reuse the reimbursements pattern):** a team leader (group chair or leader) schedules and messages *their own* team through a live seat check, not a church-wide key. This is the "approver is a seat" precedent. Phase 3 rules it, but the operator should confirm it (Q8).

Preferred approach: a **Worship Leader** role template (like the Reimbursement Clerk and Hiring Committee templates) that the administrator grants through the Roles page, with no change to the founding bundle (Q9).

- **Per-person visibility:** a roster that shows teammates' phone numbers or emails follows per-field directory privacy. It is not a new tier.
- **Rollback plan:** turning the toggle off hides every worship page and deletes nothing (the reimbursements copy precedent). Uploaded files stay private and unreachable while the toggle is off.

## Gaps the Request Didn't Address

**G1. "Worship" is three products.** The request is about the music team. FPCW's module is liturgical roles. Bulletins are for everyone. Recommendation: one spine (service plan, team, position, assignment), with a liturgical team and a praise band as two teams.

**G2. Groups cannot do this today.** Managed groups are admin-only (`groups.manage`, `/admin/groups`). A member sees only group *names* in the "Yours" card, and a member-facing groups page "would get its own `groups.view`" (`src/lib/org-portal/my-groups.ts`). A group has a roster and a role (chair, leader, member), but **no positions, no per-date assignments, no availability and no messaging.** "Extend and reuse groups" is right for roster, leader and audience. Positions, qualifications, assignments and blockouts are new tables. They should not be columns on `group_memberships`, which is end-dated and carries derived-roster guards, public listing and composite FKs. The `choir` and `team` group types already exist. Group types are a global closed catalog (DECISION-151), so do not add a type per ministry.

**G3. "Sessions" collides with the church court.** The request says "schedule sesssions". In a Presbyterian product, **Session** is the governing body, and the Court Is Not a Group is an invariant. Never use "session" in the UI for rehearsal. Q18 asks the operator to confirm it meant rehearsals.

**G4. Who may schedule?** Key or seat. See Permissions. Without a decision, either every leader needs a church-wide key (over-broad) or only the director can schedule (a bottleneck).

**G5. A response path for people who don't sign in.** Accept and decline need an account. Many choir members will not sign in. A one-click link in the email would be a **new credential class**, a single-use hashed token that authorizes only its own act, which is the DECISION-147 and DECISION-183 mechanism and needs its own security review. Recommendation: Inc 1 is sign-in only plus "leader records a response on their behalf" (attributed), and a one-click link is a later follow-up (Q10).

**G6. Conflicts and availability.** Same person, same time, two positions. Same person on two teams that overlap. Blocked out. The request mentions none. Warn, don't block.

**G7. Guests and non-members.** A hired organist or a visiting preacher is not on the roll. FPCW kept external-participant records. Recommendation: a free-text guest name on a position in Inc 1 (display and bulletin only, no contact data). A person on record who is a participant is already schedulable (Q11).

**G8. Minors.** Youth musicians are common. presby has a children model but minors typically have no account or email. Recommendation: a minor can be on a roster and scheduled. Notices to under-18s are not sent directly. The leader sees "reach via guardian". Names of minors never appear on a public surface (Q12).

**G9. Copyright and the platform's posture** (the biggest risk). The platform would store and serve a church's copyrighted charts, sheet music and recordings. Rules to design around (all from the verified section above):
- The platform is a conduit for **a church's own licensed copies, nothing else.** No platform-supplied song content and no scraped lyrics.
- **No cross-church shared song catalog or file dedup.** One church's upload becoming available to another is distribution outside the first church's license. The blob store already dedups per org and audience, so keep it that way (`blob_assets_org_audience_hash_key`).
- The license cannot be assigned, so each file belongs to exactly one church's license.
- **A DMCA designated agent and a takedown process** are needed, plus terms that put responsibility on the church. This needs counsel. I am not a lawyer, and this document is not legal advice.
- A church attestation ("my congregation holds a license covering these files") recorded with who and when, and audited.

**G10. Destroy-on-lapse versus "all deletes are soft."** The Church Copyright License requires destroying copies when it lapses. The operator rule says removals end-date, never hard-delete. The reconciling shape is a tombstone: purge the bytes, keep the metadata row (Q5).

**G11. Storage.** Audio and multi-page PDFs exceed the 10 MB cap. The 4.5 MB body cap blocks upload through a server action. Audio also needs HTTP range requests (iOS will not play it otherwise). This is an architect dependency question for Inc 5, and it changes `check:treasury-boundaries`-style tripwires. It is the reason files are not Inc 1.

**G12. Audio under the Rehearsal License.** It covers a music director sharing reference copies with the team, as streams or downloads, not retained. So audio must be **team-only** (never visible to members at large), default **stream-only**, and each distribution is reportable. The license also bars "recording your own version for distribution." A church's own band recordings may need CCLI's read (Q6).

**G13. Bulletins.** Several gaps:
- **Who is named.** "Liturgist: Jane Doe" on an open-web page needs the person's consent. Minors are never named.
- **Pastoral content.** Hospitalizations, prayer lists, deaths and birthdays are tier-3 or demographic data. They must never be auto-inserted. Anything on the bulletin is typed by the staff member.
- **Lyrics on the public web.** The base license covers *printing* lyrics in bulletins. It grants no public-performance right and bars distribution outside of services. Posting lyrics on a public page is not clearly covered, so the website copy has **titles, writers, CCLI number and © line only.**
- **Other copyrighted text.** Scripture translations and prayers from published worship books carry their own permission terms. The platform supplies none of this text. The church types it, and a standard attribution field exists.
- **Accessibility.** A large-print output is table stakes, since congregations print large-print copies today.
- **Format and the public site.** Print-optimized HTML with "save as PDF" needs no new dependency. A generated PDF would be a dependency (Phase 2). Publishing to the site follows the `liveSlot` mechanism (`presby-site-kit` v3.5.0) and a single anonymous definer read, as the careers and events slots do, with a published-state re-check at read time.

**G14. Messaging infrastructure.** No bulk or group mail exists. The email audience registry (`src/lib/contact-methods/email-audience.ts`) forces every sender to classify which address it uses, and CI fails on an unclassified key. Scheduling notices to volunteers probably want `person:personal`, not `person:role` (reimbursement notices use `person:role` because approvers are staff). Church-originated mail also has **unresolved Track F Increment 2 decisions** (a second Resend team, bounce thresholds, "decide Q13 and Q4 before real church mail", TODO line 38). Free-text team email must wait for them.

**G15. Events overlap and the signups pipeline.** A service and a rehearsal should be `events` rows. - **2FA gate.** An emailed deep link to a request must survive the `/totp` detour and return (via `/launch`). The `callbackUrl` or `next` must be validated as a same-origin path. A user mid-enrolment is redirected, not shown the request.
